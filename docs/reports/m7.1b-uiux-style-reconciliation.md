## 1. Option (A) — Still-retro-but-clearer

**Design intent:** the client is very likely reacting to the muddy 16×16
crush (chick-idle was already flagged borderline-muddy at the GO/NO-GO gate,
per R15/PLAN §7.10), not rejecting the pixel-art identity itself. (A) keeps
DESIGN §0's "Arcade Pixel" identity but gives the source model enough grid
resolution and palette headroom to render the subject clearly, while still
snapping to a discrete, quantized, mostly-hard-edged grid.

### 1.1 Revised native grid sizes (per asset category)

| Category | Current (M7.1, DESIGN/manifest as shipped) | Proposed (A) | Rationale |

### 1.2 Revised anti-aliasing / edge-softness rule (replaces Global Rule 5 + A3 check #1, DRAFT for client sign-off only)

**Revised Global Rule 5 (draft):**
> `32x32-grid 2D pixel art game sprite` (grid size per-asset per the table
> above), `retro NES/16-bit-plus arcade style, clear and legible silhouette,
> flat per-pixel color fill with discrete shading bands for form, thick dark
> outline, orthographic 2D side view, no dithering, no heavy soft shadow, no
> smooth gradient blur, no painterly rendering.` **Controlled edge softening
> is allowed in exactly one place:** a single blended transition pixel row is
> permitted on the OUTER curved/diagonal silhouette boundary only (where the
> subject's outermost color meets transparency) — never between two internal
> flat-fill color regions, and never on a straight/orthogonal edge.

**Revised A3 check #1 (draft, replaces "zero anti-aliasing / any soft edge
FAILs"):**
> **Hard edges with one bounded exception (mandatory, all assets, PROPOSED
> under style A).** Zoom to 800%. PASS = the overwhelming majority of color
> transitions — every internal boundary (comb/beak/eye/wing/window/wheel,
> any edge between two flat-fill regions) and every straight/orthogonal
> silhouette edge — are single hard steps, zero blended pixel row. The
> **only** tolerated softening is a single blended pixel row on the
> outer curved/diagonal silhouette boundary (subject-to-transparency edge).
> FAIL = more than one blended pixel row anywhere, OR any blended edge on an
> internal/functional boundary, OR softening on a straight/orthogonal edge,
> OR softening on a ground-tile's internal texture (tiles keep the stricter
> old rule — see 1.4).

**Mechanically, this is realized in the Rule 2 post-process, not by asking
the model to "just be a bit softer" (that's unreliable/unverifiable at
generation time):** insert one new optional step **before** the
nearest-neighbor downscale — a single light pre-blur (radius ≈0.5–0.75px,
one box-blur pass) applied to the raw model output. Downscaling a
pre-blurred source with nearest-neighbor naturally leaves a soft 1px ring at
curved edges (instead of a jagged stair-step) while internal flat regions
that were already uniform in the raw output stay hard after quantization.
The downscale step itself remains nearest-neighbor-only (never bilinear/
bicubic) — the softening comes from the optional pre-blur, not the resize
algorithm, keeping this fully deterministic and repeatable in our own code
exactly like today's Rule 2.

### 1.3 Revised palette / color-count guidance

Today's per-asset hex locks are intentionally tight (chicken = 7 unique
hexes total incl. outline, most vehicles = 4-5, most parallax units = 1 flat
fill, tiles = 2-3). Under (A), recommend roughly **+60-100% more colors**,
added specifically as **discrete shading bands** (never a literal smooth
gradient/blend — each added color is still one flat quantized region, just
more regions):

| Category | Current colors | Proposed (A) | How the extra colors are used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chicken | 7 | **12-14** | +1 shadow/highlight tone per major region (cream body, tan underside, comb, beak/legs, wing patch) for soft rounded form, no literal gradient. |
| Vehicles | 4-5 (body, outline, window, wheel, [taillight]) | **7-9** | +body highlight band (top-lit) +body shadow band (underside) +window reflection tint. |
| Parallax/background silhouettes | 1 flat fill | **3-4** | base + a lighter "sun-catching" band near the crest/top + a darker shadow band near the base — 2-3 discrete bands read as gentle dimensional shading without becoming a literal blurred gradient (the "no gradient" negative-prompt intent is kept at the *band* level, not violated). |
| Ground tiles | 2-3 (base, alt-shade, detail) | **4-6** | +1 extra mid-tone shading pass on the baked texture detail (e.g. a 3rd blade-tick shade on grass) — kept modest since tiles are the strictest category (1.4). |

Palette-quantize (Rule 2c) stays a **hard nearest-color snap** to these
expanded lists — the mechanism doesn't change, only the target list size.
ΔE ≤ 2 tolerance (A3 check #2) is unchanged.

### 1.4 Ground tiles stay the strictest category under (A)

Tiles are full-screen-width, at-rest, constantly-visible textures (PLAN Risk
R13) — recommend they get the **smallest** softening allowance of any
category: keep the *old, stricter* zero-AA rule for tiles specifically
(no 1px softening exception at all, since a tile has no "outer curved
silhouette" to apply it to in the first place — it's an edge-to-edge
repeating fill). Only the native-grid bump (16→32) and the modest +1-2 color
allowance (1.3) apply to tiles under (A); the edge-softness relaxation in
1.2 is scoped to sprites/parallax units, not tiles. This is a deliberate,
narrower reading of "clearer" for the one category where softening risks
undermining the seamless-tiling check (A3 check #4) rather than helping
readability.

## 2. Option (B) — Fully smooth / illustrated

Presented at the level PM asked for (directional, not a full re-spec — B is
the alternative the client can still pick, but doesn't need production-ready
prompts unless confirmed).

- **Native grid:** no fixed low-res grid at all. Treat generated art as
  regular raster illustration — recommend a working ceiling of **~128×128**
  for hero character sprites (chicken, eagle) and proportionally for others,
  purely to bound the base64 inline payload (Risk R11), not for a pixel-grid
  reason.
- **Anti-aliasing/edge rule:** drop Rule 2b/2c's downscale+quantize almost
  entirely. Keep ONLY the chroma-key transparency step (Rule 2a) — the
  model's natural smooth anti-aliased output IS the final look. No
  nearest-neighbor forcing, no "hard 1px edge" language in the style prefix
  at all.
- **Color count:** effectively unlimited/continuous. Replace the tight
  per-asset hex LOCK with a looser per-asset hex **guide** (~20-40 reference
  colors as a mood/hue-family anchor, e.g. "warm cream-and-tan feather
  tones, red comb accent") with no hard ΔE snap — palette adherence (A3
  check #2) would need to become a "stays within the stage's hue family"
  visual judgment call rather than an exact-match check.

## 3. THE CRUX QUESTION — world/chrome consistency

**Restating what stays hard-edged regardless of (A) or (B):** HUD text
(offscreen low-res buffer, DESIGN §1.6), all particles (dust/feather/splash/
sparkle — `fillRect`/polygon primitives, DESIGN §5), screen-shake/slow-mo
flash overlays, panel chrome (notched corners, hard offset shadows, DESIGN
§1.3/1.4), and any sprite that falls back to the programmatic `PIXEL_MAPS`
path (Global Rule 8, fallback-first) — all drawn via canvas primitives with
`ctx.imageSmoothingEnabled = false`. None of this is generated raster art,
and nothing in this proposal touches it.

### Under (A): dial the softening back — deliberate, bounded, still unified. DECISION: yes, dial it back; a bounded split is acceptable, an unbounded one is not.

The 1.2 rule already IS the dial-back: it caps softening to a single 1px
transition on outer curved edges only, everywhere else stays perfectly hard.
I judge this stays visually unified with the hard-edged chrome/particle
layer, for three concrete reasons:

1. **This project already has one sanctioned soft exception** (the headlight/
   streetlight/train-headlamp glow, Global Rule 6) sitting inside an
   otherwise fully hard-edged world, and it does not read as a different
   product — it reads as a deliberate, narrow accent. The 1.2 rule is the
   same shape of exception (narrow, bounded, edge-case-only), just applied
   to silhouette curves instead of light sources.
2. **A resolution/color-count split between "world" and "chrome" is a real,
   known convention from the era this style references** — 16-bit consoles
   routinely rendered higher-resolution/higher-color-depth sprites in the
   game world than their (deliberately blockier, more legible-at-a-glance)
   HUD overlays used. Resolution and color-count are NOT the axis that has
   to match for the product to read as one thing; **edge hardness is** —
   and 1.2 keeps edge hardness ~95%+ consistent (only the outer silhouette
   curve softens, comb/eye/wing-patch/window/wheel boundaries and all
   straight edges stay exactly as hard as the HUD/particles).
3. **The one thing that would actually break unity** is if "softer" were
   interpreted loosely as a general blur/gradient treatment across the whole
   sprite (which is closer to (B)) — that is explicitly what 1.2/1.4 forbid.
   As specified, (A) is closer to "same hard-edged language, more pixels to
   work with" than "a softer aesthetic," which is exactly why it stays
   compatible with the chrome layer without also having to redesign the
   chrome.

**Net: under (A), no chrome/HUD/particle restyle is needed.** The split
between crisp-pixel UI and slightly-higher-res-but-still-mostly-hard world
art is an intentional, bounded design choice, not an oversight.

### Under (B): the split becomes a real identity conflict. DECISION: flag it — B should either come with a matching chrome restyle (bigger, separate scope) or be discouraged.

Under (B) the softening is not bounded — the whole world becomes smooth/
painterly while the HUD score counter, corn count, panels, and every
particle effect stay literal hard 1px pixel blocks with zero anti-aliasing.
This is a much louder mismatch than (A)'s: a crisp 8-bit score readout
floating over a softly-shaded illustrated background/character reads as an
**unintentional** two-different-products collision (like a retro-styled UI
bolted onto an unrelated mobile-game-icon illustration), not a deliberate
stylistic juxtaposition — deliberate "mixed media" execution (some real
games do combine a retro HUD with painted art, on purpose) requires its own
considered typography/panel/shadow treatment to bridge the two, which is
exactly the kind of full identity-change work DESIGN §0 was written for, not
something that falls out of an asset-only pass.

**Recommendation if the client leans (B):** treat it explicitly as its own,
larger milestone — a full chrome/HUD/particle restyle to match, with its own
DESIGN.md identity section, token revision, and review — **out of scope for
a quick sign-off**, not something uiux should silently attempt as a
side-effect of an asset swap. PM should present this trade-off to the client
alongside the visual A/B: picking (B) does not mean "swap the art," it means
"restart the visual-identity brief." Given that cost, and that (A) already
directly answers the client's stated complaint ("too hard pixel edges" /
"can't see the object clearly") without a second identity project, **this
proposal continues to side with PM's recommendation of (A)** — (B) is kept
on the table only because it's the client's genuine call to make, not
because it's the cheaper or lower-risk path.
